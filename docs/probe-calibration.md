# Probe and anchor calibration

Device → **Anchors** offers **Auto calibrate Anchor 1** on the Z1 and **Calibrate 3D probe diameter**. Opening a guide, editing settings and preparing a plate send no machine commands. Run stays on Job; saving an anchor is a separate reviewed write.

## Contact verification and execution

Anchor 1 uses a machine-owned, bounded contact sequence instead of the firmware's long native inside-corner top search. The host requires an actual released → triggered → released probe-input transition at the stationary near-surface starting position. It binds the check to the current connection, machine XYZ, active probe tool and measured tool offset. Moving, changing tools, sending other commands or reconnecting invalidates it. The check expires after 120 seconds and is consumed once, immediately before execution. A checkbox or a successful tool-setter measurement cannot supply it.

Every contact search requires another fresh released probe-input read between two matching stationary status reports. The host sends only one NC line at a time, requires its acknowledgement and motion drain, and correlates each successful contact with that search's directed distance and fresh stopped machine coordinates (0.003 mm tolerance). An unsuccessful, missing, duplicate, malformed or inconsistent report stops the routine before the next coordinate change or travel. It halts on execution failure, never retries, and cannot Pause/Resume between contacts; **Stop** remains available. The final result requires exactly six successful contacts. Height compensation is explicitly disabled before contacts.

This is a software guard, not a guarantee against damage: search bounds limit commanded travel, not probe compression. An incorrect estimated gap, displaced bracket, signal failure after checking or unsuitable probe can still cause a collision. The bounded routine and its telemetry tolerance have not been verified on physical hardware. A simulator models contact geometry; it cannot validate wiring, real clearance, stopping distance or accuracy.

Unbounded native probing, old calibration drafts, altered bounded sequences and direct file/macro playback cannot bypass these checks on a physical connection. Ordinary tool measurement and machining remain available. Exported NC does not carry the host's contact checks and must not be run through another controller as a substitute.

The collision investigation found that the probe's USB lead was unplugged. The successful tool-setter measurement used a separate input. In the pinned firmware source, the native inside-corner cycle's first G38.2 search uses the configured tool-rack distance (default 108 mm), not the side-depth parameter. The new Anchor 1 sequence does not invoke that routine. The exact installed firmware version remains unconfirmed.

## Anchor 1

1. Home the machine. Secure the supplied L-bracket in its normal pinned Anchor 1 position, with clean top and inner faces. Select the fitted variant (thick, 15 mm tall, or thin, 5 mm tall) and the undamaged XYZ probe's own library entry, with tip diameter 0.5–3.2 mm. The guide adds the selected bracket to its plate at the front-left anchor, replacing inherited variants.
2. Insert the 3D probe securely and connect its signal cable. If it is newly fitted or remounted, **Prepare tool-setter detection**, then review and Run it on Job and follow the machine's prompts. This separate job selects and measures the probe at the setter and retracts to clearance; it does not position above the bracket. Dismiss the completed result. If the same fitted probe is already measured and active, reuse its existing measurement. No second tool-setter cycle is included in the anchor-contact job.
3. Close the guide and manually jog the probe over the solid top at the bracket's front-left elbow, clear of screws and holes. Aim 5 mm left and 5 mm forward of the inner corner, with the tip roughly 5 mm above the aluminium top. The guide shows the expected machine XY (**Anchor 1 + X−5, Y−5 mm**) and accepts a manually positioned start within 1 mm in each axis. Reopen the guide; selections remain while the app is open. This is fine calibration and still needs an approximately correct stored anchor.
4. Enter the **Estimated gap above bracket top** (0.1–5 mm) and the separate **Maximum left search · X−**, **Maximum front search · Y−** and **Maximum downward search · Z−** distances. These are total travel from each search's start, limited by the app to 0.4–10 mm, not a specification of safe probe deflection. Defaults are a 5 mm estimated gap and 6 mm limits. Downward search must exceed the estimated gap; the gap is also used for the nominal preview. Estimate conservatively, and verify the real clearances: a missing signal or wrong gap/geometry can still damage the probe. Never increase a limit automatically after a failed search.
5. With the machine stationary and spindle stopped, use the guide's live-read button with the probe released, then gently triggered and held, then released again. All three actual input states must be observed, with the selected probe active and its existing offset unchanged. Confirm the fitted probe, manually positioned start, limits and clear route, then **Prepare bounded calibration**. Review and Run it alone within two minutes, without moving or changing the probe. An expired or invalidated check requires a new check and a newly prepared calibration.
6. The first top search uses the entered maximum downward distance at 30 mm/min; the second top touch is limited to 0.4 mm at 15 mm/min. It retracts 5 mm above the touched top, travels 10 mm into the pocket along each axis, descends beside the inner walls and takes two contacts on each wall. Side depth is at least 2 mm and at least the ball radius plus 0.5 mm. First side searches use the entered X/Y limits; second touches are limited to 0.4 mm. X is released and returned clear before Y. Every search must trigger and match the commanded movement and stopped machine coordinates before any subsequent travel or coordinate change. These six contacts set the active work origin.
7. The completed result offers **Save as Anchor 1**, locked to that target. Review old/new machine coordinates before **Write anchors**. The existing acknowledged, read-back-verified writer preserves the other anchors' offsets, so they move with Anchor 1. Reset and re-home after saving so firmware-owned moves use the new configuration. The guide never resets, homes or writes automatically.

The preview starts at the checked XY and modeled bracket top plus estimated gap, with the retained probe and its radius. Its Z is nominal geometry, not a measurement of stylus length. The protected contact program has no tool change, clearance retract or added work-origin preamble before the first short search. It cannot be combined, remapped or preceded by a Stop-before. Editing the probe, diameter, bracket, original anchors or target invalidates it. Saved jobs from another connection cannot reuse the input check. Only a complete attributed result from the original device and unchanged anchor configuration can save the anchor.

This is fine calibration: the stored anchor must already be close enough to position above the correct solid bracket top and to move into its pocket. It cannot discover a misplaced bracket or recover unknown machine coordinates. It does not measure probe diameter or spindle alignment. The workflow is informed by [GabrielMaker's Anchor 1 calibration video description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4rQWrt5rDqo); contact execution is owned by the app rather than native M480.8.

The separate **3D Probe Rod** needs its own library dimensions and a conductive contact path from the contacted faces to the aluminium table through pins, screws or another connection. A metal-looking or coated bracket is not proof of conductivity. Use a connected conductive contact for the stationary input check, and verify the actual bracket faces too. Do not reuse the broken mechanical probe's entry or use the damaged probe for verification. See [Makera's Z1 Quick Start, 3D Probe Rod](https://wiki.makera.com/en/Z1/QuickStart).

## Effective probe diameter

Physical job preparation in this guide remains simulator-only because the native Boss-X routine has not been replaced by bounded, individually checked contacts. Pasted-reading calculation and report export remain available.

The four-step guide implements the known-width Boss-X method supplied with [GabrielMaker's diameter calibration video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rj9dhoIGyUQ) and the user-supplied **3D Touch Probe Diameter Calculator - GabrielMaker.html**. The calculator's scripts are not embedded or executed. Pasted commands are parsed as data and are never sent to the machine.

1. Secure a reference with a known width along machine X, clear of the machine edges. The supplied bracket is nominally 15.000 mm wide; use a measured reference value when available. Do not leave it at its normal Anchor 1 position: the probe needs space outside both sides. The measured side faces are perpendicular to X.
2. Select the physical XYZ probe, fit and cable it, home and test the probe signal. Start over the reference center, above its top. Defaults are 10 mm X offset, 2.5 mm side depth and initial D2.000. The offset must exceed half the width plus ball radius plus 1 mm. Check the entire route, including screws and clamps.
3. Prepare reading 1, Run it on Job and reopen the guide **before Dismiss**. **Collect completed reading** uses the frozen run, its originating device, its probe and the attributed complete Boss-X contacts. Each successive job uses the previous reading's effective diameter, rounded to three decimals. The guide uses a separate calibration probe library entry; it leaves the original entry unchanged until Save. Reposition over the same reference center before each Run. Closing the guide retains its draft while the app is open; it is not an automatic background job.
4. Review all three readings, confirm that they are separate physical cycles on the selected probe/reference, and save the final effective diameter to the original probe. Save updates its diameter and appends calibration notes as an undoable library edit. Future generated probing NC takes that library diameter; already exported programs and a frozen Run do not change. Export the JSON report to retain raw contacts, inputs, provenance and results before closing the app. Saved diameter and notes persist through the normal tool-library persistence.

Manual mode accepts one complete MDI log for each reading, including a single **M480.10 D… X… Y0 Z…** command and exactly six successful PRB reports after it: two top, two left and two right. Earlier TLO contacts are ignored because they precede that command. Ambiguous, mixed, failed and incomplete routines are rejected. Native collection also rejects reused run IDs, edited parameters and different devices. Editing a simulator reading does not remove its practice-only status. Pasted logs have operator-supplied provenance, which the app cannot verify.

The calculation follows the supplied calculator: left mean = average of both left contacts; right mean = average of both right contacts; contact span = right mean − left mean; effective diameter = span − known width. The result is the average of three effective diameters, rounded to 0.001 mm. For example 16.9735 − 15.0000 = 1.9735 mm, which rounds to D1.974. This differs intentionally from normal feature measurement, which uses the slower contact on each side.

Application checks require effective diameter 0.5–10 mm, side Y and Z spread at most 0.01 mm, at most 0.1 mm between the two X contacts on each side, and a final three-reading diameter range at most 0.02 mm. These are screening thresholds, not accuracy guarantees. Simulator readings can exercise the guide but cannot be saved as physical calibration. This is scalar X-derived compensation; Makera also applies that diameter to Y, whose response is not measured here. Keep the same stylus, mounting orientation and probing conditions; recalibrate after changing them. Effective diameter is compensation, not a claim that the physical ball changed size.

## Manual verification

- Inspect both bracket variants at the front-left anchor. The optional tool-setter job must measure and retract without travelling to the bracket. The contact preview must start at the manually checked XY and the modeled top plus estimated gap, and show the probe radius at its inner walls. Changing an X/Y/Z limit must change only that first search distance; removing limits from an old draft must require recreating it.
- Without commanding motion, verify that a permanently released input cannot complete the three-state check. Moving, changing tools, reconnecting, another command or expiry must invalidate a completed check. A failed check cannot be replaced with a confirmation checkbox.
- Inspect protected NC: no M6 or native M480, no added preamble/retract, one standalone operation and six bounded searches. Old drafts, changed reference geometry, tool remapping, multiple operations or changed anchors must block generation. Missing, failed or inconsistent contacts must never produce an anchor-save result.
- Simulator and pasted-reading diameter calculations remain practice/calculation workflows: reject incomplete, malformed or reused readings, and do not save simulated readings as physical calibration.
- Static build checks do not verify real stopping, wiring or accuracy. Physical verification of the new sequence is still outstanding; do not use the damaged probe for it.
